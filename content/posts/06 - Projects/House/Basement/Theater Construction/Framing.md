# Goal

Overall, half of the proposed theater will have walls against foundation. The other half (the back of the theater) will have walls adjacent to a hallway to the guest room and to a large open entertainment space. My goal is to have some degree of sound isolation from the theater to the rest of the basement as well as upstairs. I hope to achieve this by having an independently framed "room within a room" for the theater space. This will result in having a double stud wall for the boundaries of the room that would share a connection with the other parts of the basement. For the boundaries adjacent to the foundation, this won't be needed. The obvious constraints to the room are the concrete foundation and the support beams/columns. Here is the raw space I will be working with:\
![Pasted image 20260903122534.png](/ob/99%20-%20Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260903122534.png)

# Overview

In keeping with a soundproof-focused design, I'll want the inner stud walls to be isolated from the rest of the framing in the basement. Ideally, the inner stud walls would be isolated with decoupling channels from the trusses/beams above as well. The inner stud wall will be decoupled from the foundation and outer stud walls by a 1" air gap. There should be no solid connection between the inner stud wall and the foundation or outer stud wall except as needed per code (i.e. fireblocking -- hopeful to use rockwool or something similar rather than a rigid connection). There should be a cut out of the stud walls at the point of intersection by the horizontal support beam. A soffit will be created here to keep the beam out of the decoupled space. The measurements of the space in the pictures below account for drywall rather than stud-to-stud measurements.

# Walls

## Decoupled Framing

Rather than directly attach the stud plates of the inner stud wall to the floor joists, I'd like to decouple them with [IB-3](https://shop.soundproofingcompany.com/products/ib-3-decoupling-bracket?variant=33155431891079) decoupling brackets (or similar product). There should be at least a **1"** gap from the top plate to the floor joists. This will help disconnect sound transmision from the theater room to the trusses above. Outer stud walls can be connected however. Connection of bottom plate to concrete slab can be whatever.\
![Pasted image 20260902165849.png](/ob/99%20-%20Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260902165849.png)

## Separator Walls

For the boundary of the room that will share a connection with adjacent rooms, I will do double stud walls. The inner stud wall will be continuous with the stud wall adjacent to the concrete foundation. The outer stud wall will be the framing for the adjacent rooms. There should be no rigid connection between the stud walls. With the back of and side of the rooms, the outer wall will end up being underneath the steel support beams. Having the inner stud wall decoupled from the outer wall will be vital in preventing sound transmission throughout the house.\
![Pasted image 20260906142339.png](/ob/99%20-%20Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260906142339.png)![Pasted image 20260903143152.png](/ob/99%20-%20Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260903143152.png)

## Insulation

Standard fiberglass R-13 insulation in all stud walls, preserving the air gap between them. May need to use Rockwool or similar insulation to act as fireblocks as required per code within the air gap.

# Ceiling

Above the space are 2x10 trusses running from front to back. Towards the front half, a steel support beam runs perpendicular underneath the trusses (4" width, 8" height). From slab to trusses, the room height is **8' 9.5"**. Under the beam, this height drops to **7' 11.5"**. Ideally, in a "room within a room" concept for soundproofing, I would put separate joists up that would rest atop the inner stud wall (to be isolated from the outer structure). However, the presence of the support beam complicates this since I can't run them in parralel just offset of the existing joists. I could use 2x6s and run the new trusses parallel to the support beam with a soffit around the beam instead. Potentially I'd be looking at a drop in overall height to floating ceiling joists of about **8' 3.5"** (8'9.5" minus at least 0.5" air gap minus 5.5" of a 2x6). But this method may be complicated, and I'm not sure how good using 2x6s as trusses would be with a plan of supporting double 5/8" drywall in the ceiling. Alternatively, I would just use sound isolation clips on the existing joists and hang hat channels to decouple the drywall instead.

## Insulation

R-19 insulation inbetween the joists. Could consider adding drywall to the underside of the subfloor, but that would be mainly to block sound from above, which is minimal.

## Soffit

Need to create a soffit around the support beam. It is important to have the soffit decoupled from the rest of the basement structure to avoid sound passing up to the rest of the house. The easiest way would be to hang the soffit framing from the hat channels attached to the trusses (like the rest of the ceiling). The framing could attach at the ends to the inner stud walls (since that's already decoupled). OSB would be used as the first structural layer of the soffit, which would allow a single layer of drywall to be attached directly.\
![Pasted image 20260906142030.png](/ob/99%20-%20Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260906142030.png)

Would be a similar idea to how they created this soffit:\
![Pasted image 20260906142121.png](/ob/99%20-%20Attachments/Pasted%20image%2020260906142121.png)

This would allow the framers to place something up first so that drywall can easily just be placed over the soffit when the ceiling drywall is going up. Would put insulation around the support beam within the soffit.

## Projector Support

Rather than creating a hole in the ceiling to mount the projector to the wood studs, I could use plywood as the first layer on the hat channels in the back of the room, then drywall for the second layer. This would allow attaching the projector mounting bracket directly to the plywood through the drywall, rather than struggling to find a hat channel to hit and risk short-circuiting the isolation by going into a stud above:\
