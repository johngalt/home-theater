Ideal space would be in corner of basement under primary bedroom. The planned "front" of the theater would be against the concrete foundation.

# Framing

## Walls
[Reference](https://www.soundproofingcompany.com/soundproofing-solutions/soundproof-walls/spc-solution-3-double-stud-wall)
Code likely calls for rigid foam (1") between concrete foundation and stud framing. That plus 2x4 and 1/2" drywall leaves about a 5" wall around the exterior perimeter of the basement. Interior divider walls don't need that, so they would just be a 2x4 and 1/2" drywall on exposed sides. But this wouldn't be ideal for a theater room, I need to sacrifice some space to get better soundproofing. 

{{< figure src="2.png" width="782" >}}

Without any adjustments for soundproofing or extra drywall, the working dimensions would be:
- Width: 13' 8"
- Depth: 18' 2"
- Height: 7' 11" (not including soffit around support beam)

Now if we go all-in with sound proofing, we lose a little more space. Ideally, you will want double 5/8th drywall against the stud walls. For the walls against the concrete foundation, a single stud wall against 1" rigid foam will be sufficient. For walls that will share space with other rooms, I'd want to do a double stud wall with a 1/2" air gap in between as well as double 5/8th drywall on the exposed surfaces. Fortunately I can push some of this lost space out towards the other rooms rather than in towards the center of the theater. 

{{< figure src="3.png" width="782" >}}

That leaves a usable space of:
- Width: 13' 6 1/2"
- Depth: 17' 8"

As a lesser soundproofing alternative, you could just do 1 stud wall and use soundproofing clips and channels on the single stud wall (about 1 1/8” in depth for clips/channels). Honestly, it may not help save much space in the theater since with the double stud wall method, I am pushing that space out into the other rooms. With the clips and channels, it may end up shrinking the theater width a bit.

## Ceiling
[Reference](https://www.soundproofingcompany.com/soundproofing-solutions/soundproof-ceilings/spc-solution-2-soundproof-ceiling)
In the theater space I have 2x10 wood trusses running from front to back, spaced about 16" apart on center. The subfloor sits ontop of the trusses. About 7'7" back from the front foundation wall, there is an RSJ beam that runs perpendicular below the trusses. The beam has a depth of 8" and a width of about 4". There is a a 2x4 between the support beam and the wood trusses. 

When planning sound proofing for the ceiling, I would ideally use IB-1 soundproofing clips and hat channels to suspend the drywall from the ceiling trusses. As with the walls, I would use double 5/8" drywall. Clips + hat channels (1 1/8" total) + double drywall (1 1/4") would bring the ceiling surface down about 2 3/8”. R-19 insulation stuffed between the trusses above the suspended ceiling. Hat channels spaced 24" on center. Clips every 48" with last clip 6" at most from end.

If I wanted to add even more sound isolation (especially from sound from upstairs), you could add double 5/8" drywall directly beneath the subfloor between the wood trusses. Not sure if I need to go this route though as there is not much foot traffic above. 
### RSJ Beam
[Reference](https://www.ikoustic.co.uk/blog/soundproof-an-rsj/), [Example Build](https://www.avsforum.com/threads/fortress-of-amplitude.2916232/page-8?post_id=56910362&nested_view=1#post-56910362), [Another idea](https://www.avsforum.com/threads/advice-needed-soffit-around-steel-beam.3315113/?post_id=63686319&nested_view=1#post-63686319)
I need to research up more on this but I'd likely be building a soffit around the beam with additional insulation. Now I have a few options:
1. Suspend the soffit from the clipped drywall flanking the beam. Using either metal framing or 2x2s, would secure directly into suspended ceiling drywall (aiming to secure into hat channel at this point as well, if possible). Put 0.5" drywall on exterior of ladder. Another 0.5" drywall suspended below ladder on both sides. So whole soffit would be floating with suspended ceiling. (More info from  [BIGmouthinDC on AVSForum](https://www.avsforum.com/posts/16675338/)). Would give **8' 7 1/8"** ceiling height. 
   {{< figure src="4.png" width="970" >}}
2. Create true "room within a room" with new trusses supported on interior walls. Would use 2x10 wood beams across in parallel with the RSJ beam (I can't run them parallel with the existing trusses because of ducting). I wouldn't need clips/channels since the ceiling is already decoupled with the wall. This also would allow for a flush double drywall ceiling. Would give about **7' 10"** ceiling height. Overall would lose a bunch of ceiling heigh. Picture below includes clips/channels above the double drywall before I realized I wouldn't need them for this option (it also dones't really show the new trusses being supported by the inner wall -- which they would be to truly decouple the ceiling).
   {{< figure src="5.png" width="970" >}}

With option 2, it seems like it would be more ideal acoustically. But my overall height would be significantly lowered in the room. I would also have a ton of dead space with the 2x10 trusses of the inner-room and then the 2x10 trusses above. 

I feel like option 1 would be the easiest/cheapest route to take. However, I worry about the acoustic impact of that RSJ beam and soffit. It sits right about where I would want my front atmos height speakers for a 10' MLP (assuming the AT screen is 24" from the front wall). I suppose I could move the front atmos speakers closer to the MLP or just move the MLP back a bit. This may be the easiest route to preserve a higher ceiling. And I think I can make things work with the atmos speakers.
